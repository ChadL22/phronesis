The name

Phronesis is a Greek term meaning “practical wisdom,” derived from learning and evidence of practical things.

Our name reflects our goal of providing free access to carefully selected resources that inform public understanding on topics at the intersection of technology, law, and policy.

Definition adapted from The Oxford Review.

What we do

We provide policymakers, technologists, and legal professionals with free access to primary and secondary technology policy resources, each hand-selected for relevance and quality.

Phronesis is a growing library, much like Westlaw and the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy were in their early days. Everything on the site has been reviewed and hand-selected by our editorial team, led by our founder, Sipho C. Langa.

What’s in the library

Resource Overview
Resource categories Research Papers Academic works including working papers, preprints, and peer-reviewed publications on technology governance and digital law.
Policy Artifacts Legislation, memos, and proposals addressed to regulators, lawmakers, and institutions.
Legal Analyses Close readings of cases, statutes, and regulatory actions. Written for practitioners and informed general readers.
Essays Works written to explain, argue, narrate, or describe ideas and positions on technology governance.
Articles Timely, concise takes on current events: rulings, decisions, and policy shifts worth paying attention to right now.
Reports Formal, organized communications that share findings from investigation, analysis, or research, tailored to a specific audience and purpose, often including recommendations or conclusions.
Topic Collections Canons Curated reading lists that gather pieces from every category on a single topic. A good place to start.
Newsletter The Phronesis Brief A monthly briefing on developments in technology law and policy, delivered by email.

Resource Acquisition Process

Although resources are generally hand-selected by our editorial team, we periodically review reader submissions for topics to explore. If there is a topic you would like us to cover, or work you believe would make a great addition to the library, we would like to hear from you.

Send topic suggestions and submissions to submissions@phronesisresearch.org.

Email us

Independence

Phronesis is independently curated. We select resources for their relevance and quality, and inclusion does not imply endorsement of an author’s views or conclusions.

We do not accept funding that gives donors control over which resources we include or how we present them.
